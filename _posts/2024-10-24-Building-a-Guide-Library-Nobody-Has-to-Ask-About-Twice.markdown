**Answering the same question for the fifth time is a sign, not just a mildly annoying ticket.**

Somewhere in the last couple of years this turned into an actual habit rather than something I do occasionally: the moment a question or a process looks likely to come up again, I write it down properly rather than just answering it well once. It sounds obvious written out like that. In practice it's easy to skip, because answering the question in front of you right now is always faster than stepping back to build something reusable.

The format depends entirely on the problem. Some things need a short written guide, step by step, screenshots included, for anyone who wants to work through it at their own pace. Some things genuinely need video, laptop setup being the clearest example, where watching someone click through the actual screens beats reading a description of where to click. Some things work best as a slide deck for a room full of people at once rather than a document one person reads alone. I've stopped assuming there's one correct format and started matching the format to how people actually want to receive that particular piece of information.

The payoff isn't dramatic on any single day. It shows up gradually, as a quieter drop in the same repeat tickets and emails that used to eat a chunk of every week. Staff confidence with day-to-day systems has genuinely gone up alongside it, not because anyone got smarter, but because the answer to a common question is now sitting somewhere findable instead of living only in my head, or worse, in an old email thread nobody can search properly.

None of this is complicated. It's just choosing, fairly often, to spend twenty extra minutes on the first instance of a problem so the fortieth instance doesn't need me at all.
