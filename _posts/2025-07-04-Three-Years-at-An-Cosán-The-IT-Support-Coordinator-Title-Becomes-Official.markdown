**Three years at An Cosán, and this is the first anniversary where my job title actually changed.**

Last year I wrote about stepping up without a formal promotion, taking on more ownership while my title stayed the same. That informal shift has now caught up with the paperwork. Following the Educational Technologist's departure, I've moved into a new role, IT Support Coordinator, with direct responsibility for day-to-day IT infrastructure operations, service delivery, and our Community Employment IT Support Team.

It's less a change in the work and more a change in what the work is called. I was already coordinating infrastructure across our seven Dublin sites and five national education hubs, already leading and mentoring the team, already reporting proposals and completion reports up to senior leadership. Having a title that matches the responsibility makes it easier to have the right conversations, about resourcing, about priorities, and about where the team needs to grow next.

Leading a five-person team properly, rather than informally, has meant putting real structure around it: allocating work deliberately, coordinating leave cover so nothing falls through the cracks, setting objectives, and running quarterly performance reviews. Coaching people is a different skill to fixing tickets, and it's one I've had to get more intentional about this year.

On the infrastructure side, I've kept pushing on the things that don't get noticed until they fail: patch and vulnerability management, access reviews, disaster recovery testing against documented recovery targets, and keeping a continuity Run Book so none of it depends on one person remembering how something was set up. None of that is glamorous, but it's the difference between a service desk that reacts to problems and one that prevents them.

A title change doesn't alter the underlying belief I keep coming back to: technology should adapt to the people using it, not the other way round. What it does give me is a clearer mandate to act on that belief at a larger scale, across more of the organisation, with more of the team behind me.
