**Most team leads measure success by keeping good people. I lead a team where the actual goal is the opposite: helping people build enough skill and confidence that they leave for something better.**

The IT Support Team I lead works through a Community Employment scheme, which exists specifically to help people build workplace experience on the way to full-time employment elsewhere. It changes what leading the team actually means day to day. This was never going to be a group I keep together for years. It's a group I owe the best possible year to, however long each person is actually here.

In practice that means the coaching has to be real, not decorative. Quarterly reviews cover the obvious things, the quality and pace of the work, but just as much time goes into how someone is actually doing, whether they're stuck, whether something outside work is making things harder, whether they're ready for more responsibility than they currently have. Delegation runs the same way I'd want to be delegated to: real ownership of real tickets, not busywork dressed up as training.

Some of the best moments haven't had anything to do with IT directly. One participant was far more interested in web work than helpdesk tickets, so rather than trying to force a fit that wasn't there, I introduced them to our communications manager and helped arrange some introductory tasks outside what the IT team would normally cover. It wasn't my project to take credit for, and that was the point. Matching someone to where their actual interest was mattered more than keeping them fully occupied on my own team's backlog.

The genuinely rewarding feedback comes later, after someone's moved on. A former team member landed a Level 1 helpdesk role elsewhere and got in touch to say the training had been immediately applicable on day one. That's the actual scoreboard for this kind of team. Not retention, not how long someone stayed, but whether what they learned here held up the moment it mattered somewhere else.

I still find it a little strange to describe good leadership as helping your best people leave. But if the team exists to build people up and send them on, then a leader who resents that is measuring the wrong thing.
