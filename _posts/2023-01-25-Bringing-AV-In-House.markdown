**My QQI in Media and Film Production has come up in exactly one job interview ever. It's come up constantly at work, just never in the way I expected.**

Staff days and graduations used to mean bringing in an external AV company: their gear, their crew, their invoice. It's a perfectly reasonable way to do it, but it's also expensive for things that happen on a predictable schedule every year, and it means the organisation has no control over the setup beyond what's specified in a booking email.

Having actually studied audio and video production before landing in IT, I could see fairly quickly where the gap was. Most of what an external company was providing for these events, a mixer, decent microphones, a way to record and project cleanly, is equipment IT could own outright and get real repeated use out of, not just once a year at a markup.

So that's what we did. Built up a small kit of our own AV equipment, learned the specific quirks of running sound and video for a graduation hall rather than a studio, and started handling smaller internal events entirely in-house. Staff days, graduations, the occasional recorded message from senior leadership, all things that used to mean an external quote now just mean booking time with our own kit.

It hasn't been flawless. Running live audio for a room full of people is a genuinely different skill to editing something after the fact with unlimited retakes, and I've had a few real-time lessons in exactly how a room's acoustics can ruin a mix you tested perfectly the day before. But every event is another rep, and the cost savings alone have made the learning curve worth it.

It's a strange kind of validation, watching a qualification I picked up out of pure interest turn into something that saves the organisation real money and gives events a level of polish they wouldn't otherwise get. I didn't see this specific use case coming, but I'll take it.
