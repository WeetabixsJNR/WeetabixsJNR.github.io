**Upgrading one site's broadband and phone system is an afternoon of coordination. Doing it across six sites at once is a project with its own gravity.**

The trigger was simple enough: ageing connections and a phone system that had outgrown what it was originally set up to handle, spread across six different locations with six different circumstances. What made it a real project rather than a string of small jobs was that none of it could happen in isolation. A shared, unified phone system across every site only works if every site actually gets there, and broadband circuits, equipment installation and staff readiness all had to line up site by site without any one location holding the rest back.

Most of the early work wasn't technical at all. Contacting providers, gathering pricing, and turning that into value-for-money comparisons that actually meant something rather than just picking the biggest name. Coordinating multiple external providers for circuits and equipment installation, each with their own scheduling constraints, against six site managers who all had their own operational calendars I couldn't just override.

The actual cutover at each site needed a staff-readiness check beforehand, because a phone system upgrade that catches a site off guard mid-week is worse than the old system limping along a little longer. That meant working site by site rather than trying to force a single big-bang switchover everywhere at once, reporting progress upward as each location came online, and being ready to produce an issue report on the spot when something at a given site didn't go the way the plan said it would.

By the end, every site was on the same unified phone system and materially better connections than they'd had before, and internal communication between sites got noticeably easier once everyone was actually on the same platform. The technical upgrade was the easy part to plan for. Getting six sites, six sets of local quirks and six different provider schedules to land on the same outcome without anyone's week getting wrecked in the process was the actual project.
